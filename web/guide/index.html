GruselCore Guide

Your loops in the slots

SLICE plays your own recordings only. It has no built-in loops: GruselCore removed Skiff's BREAK and FUNK and the PIANO source. A sound or project that used one of them plays USR1 instead.

Per slot

Files per slot

  1. Connect the FM-1 over USB and open the Skiff Editor in Chrome or Edge.

  2. On its Samples tab, drop your WAV into a slot, trim it, listen to it as the FM-1 will store it, and upload.

  3. On the FM-1, put SLICE on a track (EDIT › ENGINE), then set SOURCE to that slot and DIV to 8 or 16. AUTO puts one slice on every hit; MAN lets you set them by hand on EDIT › SLICES.

Preparing a break

  • Cut it exactly on the bar: start on the first kick, end just before the next bar's first beat. Equal slices (DIV 8, 16) only land on the hits when the loop is exact.
  • Trim the silence at both ends, and put very short fades at the cuts so they don't click.
  • Any WAV works: stereo is mixed to mono and every sample rate is converted. Make your loops about equally loud. Best: give it mono at 22,050 Hz yourself, with a good resampler; the editor then stores it as it is.
  • Two bars is the sweet spot: on a 2-bar loop, DIV 32 gives one slice per 16th (the grid's steps), DIV 16 one per 8th, and AUTO finds every hit. On a 4-bar loop AUTO stops at 32 slices, so split it into two halves.
  • Pick breaks near your song tempo. SLICE doesn't stretch to the tempo: it plays at the recording's speed, and PITCH makes it faster and higher or slower and lower together. One bar at 170 BPM lasts 1.41 s, so a slot holds about five of them.
Several loops in one slot

A slot's WAVs play back to back as one recording, so DIV cuts the whole chain, not each loop. One tidy way: four one-bar loops of exactly the same length and DIV 32. Every 8 slices are then one loop, and START 0, 8, 16 or 24 picks the loop that sits on the first keys. For EVOLVE, one break per slot is cleanest.

Licences

Classic breaks (Amen, Think, Apache …) are cut from records. They are fine on your own unit; only CC0 or CC BY loops (credited) may go into the public GruselCore repository.

Which key plays which slice

SLICE uses all 27 keys, white and black, in order from the lowest F. The first key plays slice 1, the next slice 2, and so on; after the last slice it starts again at slice 1. The octave buttons shift the keys by 12 slices, which changes which slice a key plays at DIV 8, 16 and 32. Keep the octave at 0 (both OCT lights off; OCT− + OCT+ together brings it back) and the chart below holds as shown.

KeyFF♯GG♯AA♯BC
DIV 812345678

START (EDIT page, KNOB 3) moves the slices across the keys: START 3 puts slice 4 on the lowest F.

3

The SLICE grid

On a SLICE track, SEQ › NOTES is the SLICE grid: eight slices down the side, the 16 steps of a page across. A step plays one slice or nothing. Select a step with a white key, then give it a slice with a black key. Nothing sounds while you do, so it never disturbs what is playing.

ControlWhat it does on the grid
White 1–16Select that step (its key lights up). Nothing is written yet.
Black 1–8Put that slice on the selected step, silently. A different slice there is replaced; the step's own slice again removes it.
Black 9The next eight slices (9–16, 17–24 …) at DIV 16 or 32; after the last eight, back to the first.
Black 10 / 11The page down / up, for patterns longer than 16 steps.
PRESETSMoves the selected step (the framed one), as the white keys do.
KNOB 1 SLICEThe selected step's slice, one slice per detent (after the last: the first). On an empty step: the slice of the highlighted row.
KNOB 2 RATCHThe step plays ×1 to ×4.
KNOB 3 RANDOMRight: every step that has a slice gets a new random slice, with each detent. Left: your own order comes back. No step is ever added or removed, so the rhythm stays.
KNOB 4 VELOCITYHow hard the step plays.

The selected step's white key is lit, and every step that has a slice glows brighter, so you see the pattern's rhythm. The black key of the selected step's slice is lit, and its row is highlighted on the screen. A step playing a slice of the other eights shows a short bar at the top or bottom of its column. Hold a white key and turn a knob to lock a sound setting on that step, as everywhere in Skiff.

One slice at a time

SLICE always plays a single slice. When a new slice starts, the one before is cut with a very short fade, so a slice on every step, a RATCH, or keys played fast give clean stutters, never two slices over each other. At DIV 8 a slice lasts two steps; put the next slice on the very next step and it cuts the first one short.

The loop in order: DIV 8, a slice on every second step

  1. Select the SLICE track, set DIV 8 on its EDIT page, and open SEQ › NOTES.

  2. Press white 1 (step 1), then black 1: slice 1 on step 1.

  3. Continue the same way, white key first:

    White key (step)13579111315
    Black key (slice)12345678

  4. Press PLAY. It works while playing too: tap steps in and out and hear the change on the next pass.

Ideas

  • Classic re-sequencing: select a few off-beat steps one after another and give each the snare slice.
  • Rolls: put the snare slice on steps 13–16 and give them RATCH ×2.
  • Stutters: put the same slice on two or four steps in a row; each one cuts the last, so you hear the slice's start repeated.
  • New ideas fast: set the rhythm you like, then turn RANDOM right a detent at a time until a version grabs you, or left to get your own order back. Fine-tune single steps afterwards with KNOB 1.
What "your own order" means

The grid remembers the slices as they were when you first turned RANDOM right. Left brings that back as long as you only randomize. Change a slice by hand in between, and the pattern as it is then becomes your own order.

REC is not needed

The grid writes the steps directly. REC on the grid just says "GRID: TAP THE STEPS".

The whole loop

SLICE plays one slice per note. Four ways to hear the loop in full:

SLICE, one key

Join it into one slice

On EDIT › SLICES, use JOIN (KNOB 4, then OCT+) until one slice is left. The lowest key then plays the whole loop: MODE ONE once, MODE LOOP for as long as you hold it. The slices are saved with the sample.

SAMPLE engine

Play the recording

Put SAMPLE on another track with SET USR1 (or USR2, USR3). A key plays the whole recording: at its root key at the original speed, other keys faster or slower.

SLICE, the steps

The slices in order

Put the slices in order on the SLICE grid (chapter 3). That rebuilds the loop exactly, and every step stays editable.

Before upload

The Skiff Editor

The Samples tab plays the trimmed sample as the FM-1 will store it, at 22,050 Hz.

5

EVOLVE chops the break

On a SLICE track, EVOLVE is a break generator. Hold ARP, turn PRESETS to KEYS EVOLVE and let go; F3 turns it on, G3–E4 pick the track, and KNOB 1 is that track's AMOUNT. Each new version of the pattern changes some slices; your own pattern never changes.

What AMOUNT does

EVOLVE plays a copy of your pattern and makes a new version of it every pass (or every 2, 4, 8, 16 passes: KNOB 2, EVERY). Each new version starts from the one before and does two things:

  1. Some steps go back to your pattern. Each step returns to what you entered with a chance that falls as AMOUNT rises, so a low AMOUNT keeps pulling the break home.

  2. Then a few slices change. AMOUNT sets how many: up to half the steps. Each change picks one step (never a protected beat) and one move from the table below. The mix of moves is the same at every AMOUNT.

For a 16-step pattern; a 32-step one gets twice the changes.

MoveShareWhat it does
Roll24 %The step plays the slice of the note before it again.

Jump10 %2 to 4 slices ahead.
Add10 %A slice goes into a rest 1 to 3 steps after it: the same slice or the next one.

No slice of your pattern is ever left out, and there are no ×3 or ×4 stutters: even AMOUNT 100 stays a jungle break, busy and far from the loop, never breakcore.

The other controls

  • AMOUNT off at once: hold OCT− and turn KNOB 1 to the left. The track plays its own pattern again straight away. With F4 (ALL) held too, every track does.
  • PROTECT (C5) keeps the slices on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, the loop's kick and snare when it plays in order.
  • ARRANGE (KNOB 3) adds ×2 fills on the last beat's rests, heard only in the last bar of each phrase.
  • CHORD and ROOT leave SLICE tracks alone.
  • An empty SLICE track plays its loop in order as soon as EVOLVE starts, then changes it.

Shortcut: the loop in order as a pattern

  1. Leave the SLICE track empty and start playback.

  2. Turn EVOLVE on and give the track some AMOUNT. It plays the loop in order straight away.

  3. Before the first bar ends, press D5 (FREEZE). That stops it from changing.

  4. Press A4 (KEEP). That stores the in-order pattern as a new entry in the track's list, to edit like any other.

Not yet tried on the unit

This shortcut follows from how EVOLVE works, but it hasn't been tested on an FM-1 yet; the timing may need a second try. The SLICE grid (chapter 3) is the reliable way.

Dark DnB basses

Ten sounds for dark jungle, drum and bass and neurofunk, built from the firmware's own engines and modelled on reference one-shots by measuring them: pitch, envelope, brightness over time, wobble speed. None of the reference audio is in the firmware. Find them under SAVE › PRESETS with LIST on BASS (DARK AIR: PAD).

GROWL WOBANALOGA driven growl with a fast, shallow filter wobble (~3.8 Hz); the level holds.
HOLLOW 8ANALOGA hollow, square-ish bass pulsing in 8ths with the tempo.
LASER DIVEANALOGDives in pitch as it fades, through a slow flanger: the neuro "laser".
NEURO MIDANALOGA resonant growl whose filter jumps at random in 8th triplets over a steady sub.
YOY BASSANALOGA resonant filter up, down and up again per quarter note: "yoy".
SCOOP CHOPANALOGScoops up into each note; made for short, chopped steps.
SUB DRONEANALOGA dark, slowly moving sub drone.
GATE REESETRIOA reese gated in 16ths with the tempo.
DARK AIRTRIOAn airy atmosphere: two high tones over a low one, pulsing gently.
  • The wobbles follow the tempo (HOLLOW 8, NEURO MID, YOY BASS, GATE REESE); GROWL WOB's runs at its own speed.
  • All are MONO with legato, except DARK AIR. They sit about as loud as the other basses.
  • Octave: they play at the pitch of the keys; for the deepest subs, play them an octave lower with OCT− or the track's transpose.

Scales on the keys

Every melodic track has a ROOT and a SCALE. GruselCore starts every new track in C minor (MIN; Skiff starts on CHR, every note). A saved project keeps the scale it was saved with. The scale shapes the in-key chords, the NOTES page's PITCH knob, EVOLVE's melodies and chord follow, and it can light up the keys. It changes which notes the keys play only when QNT is on (below). Drum tracks have no scale.

  1. See the scale on the keys: hold HOME and let it go for the menu, then CONTROL › SCALE LEDS › ON. The keys of the selected track's scale glow, the root's brighter; the others stay dark. It stays on and follows the track you select.

  2. Pick the root: hold SEL and press a key: its note is the ROOT (the F key sets F, and so on).

  3. Pick the scale: still holding SEL, turn KNOB 2. The screen shows all 23 scales; the minor and dark ones come first, in the top four rows.

  4. Play only the scale (optional): on the SCALE page (press SEL), set QNT to WHITE: C4 plays the root and each white key the next note of the scale, so no wrong note is possible. SNAP keeps every key but moves notes outside the scale down onto it; OFF plays every key as it is.

Older firmware

NEAP, HUNG, PDOM, DHARM, HIRA, INSEN and KUMOI are GruselCore's own. Skiff, or a GruselCore before them, opens a track set to one of them as DIMWH.
